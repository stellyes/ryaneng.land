Okay... look. This isn't a personal attack

Unless of course, you do take offense before reading a word of this, in which case _this is written about you specifically._

I'm not in a position to say that social media is going to ruin your artistic integrity. I use social media both casually and for promotional reasons. I believe I am immune to this, because I have an undying urge to create like the bus from the 1994 movie "Speed" inability to stop. That is to say that what I possess is creativity like it's a non-contagious virus and if I stop there's a likely chance that I am simply dead. I hope heaven has a recording booth.

I notice a growing trend with people online becoming artists entirely for the sake of "blowing up." What a stupid phrase "blowing up" is. There's a palpable disingenuous feeling to anything created for clout. I believe the great artists before me called this:

<h1 style="text-align: center">"poser behavior"</h1>

\- and they couldn't be more right as far as I'm concerned. Interests and hobbies stem from desire, a curiosity that is either satisfied or stays with you until it manifests into purpose. If your purpose is to expose yourself to the world by any means necessary, start an OnlyFans.

I greatly fear for the new generation of "artists" and artists alike. Will the artist be washed away by the sewage that imitates them? Or will passion persevere?

I aim to (maybe) answer this question and dive a bit into what others before me have concluded about posers being posers. More academically said,

<em>"When exposure becomes the goal of creation, will the work shift from expressing the self to performing it?"</em>

<h1><span style="font-family: Warbler; font-size: 0.8em;">I. </span>Ego Indulgence</h1>

Platforms designed to maximize engagement naturally attract people who want their slice of that attention. F

No shit. This is reinforced by a meta-analysis by Jessica McCain and W. Keith Campbell titled "Narcissism and social media use." Across 62 studies and 13,000 participants, there was found to be a reliable link between narcissistic and a range of social media behaviors, including the frequency in which people post and the number of followers they have. The authors posture that "individuals with pre-existing narcissism are drawn to social media."

I want to briefly sidebar - this seems a bit extreme to me, or maybe "Narcissism" has been used to too often in a diagnostic context for us to remember it can be used as an adjective to describe patterns, no dictate identity.
