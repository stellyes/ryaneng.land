Today, I am reminiscing on my time living in the San Francisco Bay Area. I spent some time living on the peninsula, some time in the east bay, and some time with my head up my ass. To understand how I found myself in that position, we have to go waaaaay back.

> _The year was 2018... Musical.ly was just acquired by ByteDance, or as we know it to be now, TikTok, and was making headlines for its userbase publicly embarassing themselves. Soundcloud rap was, as some say, at its
> peak, slowly meeting its fall after the passing of XXXTentacion (not that I particularly cared) and - oh - what the hell was COVID? More importantly, where was I in all of this?_

I was fooling around with my then-girlfriend by spending every penny I had on us across the Canadian border, stealing time from my warehouse job (before I got fired, for stealing time), and trying to max out my swag by using the little money I had left to purchase hypebeast garbage. For a young "adult" at the ripe age of 19 in the Pacific Northwest, I was living the dream.

This was obviously not sustainable. I eventually settled on moving in with my family down in California. A midlife crisis had influenced my parents (father) to drop everything and relocate to the suburban hellscape that is Moorpark. Located in south Ventura county, just bordering Los Angeles county, Moorpark was an interesting place to start my journey as a Californian. The population of Moorpark was that of a college town - young, broke kids and elderly folks with lots of money and poor opinions about said young people. Starting at Moorpark college and picking up a job at Jamba Juice, I was thankfully able to stick with my people.

It was at this Jamba Juice that I met the person who would assist me in blowing up my life. We can refer to them simply as O. I do not blame O for the mess that I created for myself, but like I said, I do put the label "assist" on what ended up transpiring over that rough 5 years in southern California.

What followed my tenure in SoCal was what I like to describe as metamorphosis. Painful, but transformative. My biggest blessing was that I didn't own/couldn't afford a gun, lol.

I had invested the last of my savings account into a Full Stack Web Engineering program and moving costs to relocate up to the Bay Area. My last month down south was spent eating astronaut food (no joke), and starving. When I first arrived, I ate a sandwich in Cole Valley and cried on the sidewalk. My current condition _very_ obvious to me that day. Things did not get better from this point.

I don't even want to start getting into how the Web bootcamp panned out. This was 2023, when AI had taken a hold on the market and we (software engineers) were going to be replaced in a matter of 6 months. Although this never happened, the media hype surrounding these claims basically meant that I had spent all of my money on a piece of paper.

Quick callback to what I said earlier - it's a blessing I couldn't afford a gun.

I was lucky enough to secure a job serving at this "greek" restaurant (quotations because the restaurant owner had fired the two original chefs who built the menu and vibe of the place within the first month of opening, lol). After missing my **first** month of rent, lapsing on my car payment, lapsing on my car _insurance_ payment, and still not eating a straight meal in the comfort of my own cubicle, I had amassed a whopping $600. For context, this covered my car payment and half of my car insurance. I was on the brink of collapse.

I'm thankful for the people that propped me up during this period of my life. I still don't think I've fully recovered from the emotional damage this caused me. Damage, I suppose, is a harsh word to describe how I've triumphed the experience, and instead I want to emphasize that I am much better for it in the end. I wouldn't wish the poor to homeless teeter upon my worst enemies. I know now that:

- A flower can blossom from the cracks of the concrete
- I cannot die
- You can stretch a carton of eggs, a pound of rice, 3 pounds of frozen broccoli and whatever seasoning you find in the spice cabinet for upwards of 3 weeks if you also supplement it by eating the leftovers off of the plates you buss from tables

I wish I was joking about that last one.
