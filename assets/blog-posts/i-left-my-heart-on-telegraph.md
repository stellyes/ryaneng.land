Today, I am reminiscing on my time living in the San Francisco Bay Area. I spent some time living on the peninsula, some time in the east bay, and some time with my head up my ass. To understand how I found myself in that position, we have to go waaaaay back.

> _The year was 2018... Musical.ly was just acquired by ByteDance, or as we know it to be now, TikTok, and was making headlines for its user base publicly embarrassing themselves. Soundcloud rap was, as some say, at its
> peak, slowly meeting its fall after the passing of XXXTentacion (not that I particularly cared) and - oh - what the hell was COVID? More importantly, where was I in all of this?_

<h1><span style="font-family: Warbler; font-size: 0.8em;">I. </span> Preface, and the years in Southern California</h1>

I was fooling around with my then-girlfriend by spending every penny I had on us across the Canadian border, stealing time from my warehouse job (before I got fired, for stealing time), and trying to max out my swag by using the little money I had left to purchase hypebeast garbage. For a young "adult" at the ripe age of 19 in the Pacific Northwest, I was living the dream.

This was obviously not sustainable. I eventually settled on moving in with my family down in California. A midlife crisis had influenced my parents (father) to drop everything and relocate to the suburban hell-scape that is Moorpark. Located in south Ventura county, just bordering Los Angeles county, Moorpark was an interesting place to start my journey as a Californian. The population of Moorpark was that of a college town - young, broke kids and elderly folks with lots of money and poor opinions about said young people. Starting at Moorpark college and picking up a job at Jamba Juice, I was thankfully able to stick with my people.

It was at this Jamba Juice that I met the person who would assist me in blowing up my life. We can refer to them simply as O. I do not blame O for the mess that I created for myself, but like I said, I do put the label "assist" on what ended up transpiring over that rough 5 years in southern California.

I say "assisted" to minimize direct blame. I had a propensity to completely drown myself in my romantic partnerships, as my dedication and support to these people was a good substitute for any focus inward. O had completely indulged this, and I sat idly by, like the dog on the porch waiting to be remembered. I wasted years of my life fighting for that love, and a few more years recovering (coping) from the loss of the relationship's potential.

What followed in the wake of this was something most people would describe as drug and alcohol abuse, but personally, I treated my substances with nothing but love and adoration. I was having the time of my life, despite what the rehab facility may have taught me during my stay! I look back on this time with a deep sadness for my younger self, and a grief for the time that I wasted and the days, weeks, and months I can't remember whatsoever. Benzodiazepenes are a hell of a drug. It's a miracle I lived through this considering how much I was drinking in addition.

My tenure in SoCal was what I describe as metamorphosis. Painful, but transformative, and certainly needed. My greatest blessing was that I didn't own/couldn't afford a gun, lol. I will not be elaborating further on what I mean by that.

<h1><span style="font-family: Warbler; font-size: 0.8em;">II. </span>  Metamorphosis and crawling up from Rock Bottom</h1>

I had invested the last of my savings account into a Full Stack Web Engineering program and moving costs to relocate up to the Bay Area. My last month down south was spent eating astronaut food (no joke), and starving. When I first arrived, I ate a sandwich in Cole Valley and cried on the sidewalk. My condition was _very_ obvious to me that day. Things did not get better from this point.

I don't even want to start into how the Web bootcamp panned out. This was 2023, when AI had taken a hold on the market and we (software engineers) were going to be replaced in a matter of 6 months. Although this never happened, the media hype surrounding these claims basically meant that I had spent all of my money on decorated toilet paper in exchange for three months of my time.

Quick callback to what I said earlier - it's a blessing I couldn't afford a gun.

I was lucky enough to secure a job serving at this "greek" restaurant (quotations because the restaurant owner had fired the two original chefs who built the menu and vibe of the place within the first month of opening, lol). Of course, there were hiccups in the hiring process, namely that the manager who hired me did not alert the manager in charge of scheduling... so the only time I was working was when I was picking up shifts as they were made available from folks calling out. This went on for a month.

After missing my **first** month of rent, lapsing on my car payment, lapsing on my car _insurance_ payment, and still not eating a straight meal in the comfort of my own cubicle, I had amassed a whopping $600. For context, this covered my car payment and half of my car insurance. I was on the brink of collapse.

I'm thankful for the people that propped me up during this period of my life. Damaging, I suppose, is a harsh word to describe the experience in spite of how I've triumphed it. Instead, I want to emphasize that I am much better for it in the end. I wouldn't wish the poor to homeless teeter upon my worst enemies. I know now that:

- A flower can blossom from the cracks of the concrete
- I cannot die
- You can stretch a carton of eggs, a 3 pounds of rice, a pound of frozen broccoli and whatever seasoning you find in the spice cabinet for upwards of 3 weeks if you also supplement it by eating the leftovers off of the plates you buss from tables

I wish I was joking about that last one.

<h1><span style="font-family: Warbler; font-size: 0.8em;">III. </span>  Getting up when someone kicks the back of your knee</h1>

Things eventually turned around for me (financially). Enough for me to afford that gun I kept thinking about and enough for me to not have any interest in owning a gun any longer.

The "greek" restaurant had promoted me! I was a shift lead and lead bartender, I had just built out a cocktail menu for the spring season menu change. I was going to feature my famous "Naked and Afraid" - a delicious and refreshing drink that I spent several weeks trying out against the customers at the bar-top.

Two weeks after this promotion they _SHUT THE RESTAURANT DOWN AND LET STAFF KNOW WITH NO ADVANCE, OVER TEXT._

Just as I gained financial stability, I was now facing the direction of the abyss once more. I took this loss on the chin. To my surprise, my lack of caring worked to my advantage, as things quickly picked up for me again.

Phonobar was probably the most "professional" bar job I had ever gotten. Everything prior to this operated under the mindset that if you "be flirty and attentive to the needs of guests, good things will come to you." That was still true here, but at $21 a cocktail or more, the stakes were a lot higher. The clientele weren't even different from the rooftop bar, but these were the elites that cared about being treated elite. They were young, sexy, and had a flagpole-sized stick up their asses.

I have bitter and fond memories of this place. On one hand, I barely slept for my tenure working here. The scheduling was abhorrent. The work environment was awesome, though, and I met some of the coolest people in the Bay Area at this bar. Career bartenders, artists, immigrants, partiers, and generally good people. My official review - the best bar/cocktail experience you can find in Hayes Valley is here. The bar is classy, lively, and carefully curated. The guy above, Shai, is a genius 'mixologist', as much as I hate using that identifier for people that can just make a damn-good drink.

At this point, I had moved out to Oakland, just outside of downtown. This place was fantastic for what I needed at the time.

On one hand, I lived on the top floor of a complex with a view of the San Francisco skyline, I had my own kitchen, and I was now able to experience the rich culture that exists in the East Bay. I also became familiar with BART. With this new found mobility, I explored the various public parks and hiking trails (route permitting). I found myself visiting the Albany Bulb often. There were always so many oddities at the Bulb, always in rotation from the punks that would come in at night.

On the other hand, commuting to San Francisco was easy enough, but leaving work at 3:00 AM? Take the overnight 800 AC route! It runs every hour and you only have a small chance of witnessing someone die on the bus. In my case, this happened twice. It was either that or pitch for an Uber across the bridge, which cost $50 minimum. It was a nightly gamble I had to take. Last time I checked, $3 was far less than $50. What's witnessing societal decay first-hand for a few bucks saved? That's three packs of smokes, after all...

The door guy at the bar eventually got me a job working at a dispensary, which was awesome as far as my sleep schedule was concerned.

<h1><span style="font-family: Warbler; font-size: 0.8em;">IV. </span>  From dust, I shall return</h1>

The "beginning of the end" doesn't appear obvious at this point, to anybody. This dispensary job, while paying the most out of any position I held previously, introduced me to some of the most degrading relationships I'd ever cultivate. My boss was a degenerate alcoholic father, professional drunk driver, dabbler in cocaine dealing, who's co-manager was his ex-wife. She was sweet... -ish. My coworkers were either cool folks or the most annoying people I've encountered. There was only one guy that led to a non-doc'd assault, but I won't be getting into that.

Because I've gone into extensive detail about my experience leading up to this, I'll save you the finer details and sum up my tenure at the dispensary in a pros-and-cons list:

## Pros

- Free weed. A _lot_ of free weed.
- Regular hours. 1:00 PM - 10:00 PM working hours. No getting off in the early AM.
- Being in the industry of intoxication for so many years, cannabis users have to be the easies to work with.
- I ended up dating one of my managers, which led me to be promoted within the company to Marketing Manager. This was my first desk job!

## Cons

- I ended up dating one of my coworkers...
- Cannabis industry workers (at least in California) are now-legal dealers. That is to say, they spent years operating outside of the law. Their connections may no longer be good for cannabis, but they certainly exercise their connections for the sale and distribution of other substances.
- To piggy-back off the above, there was a lot of substance abuse in the workplace. During working hours. In front of guests.
- My boss would routinely steal from his own job, to the extent it affected his margins. When he came in fuming that he was losing money, I couldn't tell him it was his own doing, and instead had to convince him others were stealing from him instead. He watch camera feeds on his phone like a hawk, but the limited brain cells remaining in him would never catch anything anyway, despite him being the culprit for his own downfall.
- Checks would routinely bounce. At least, they would bounce every three months at minimum. From a business that brought in $200,000 a month on average across it's operating time, you have to ask, _where the fuck is the money going?_

<h1><span style="font-family: Warbler; font-size: 0.8em;">V. </span>  Settled dust</h1>

From here, I lasted a total of four and a half more months in California. It's bittersweet. I spent these months revisiting old friendships, regretfully building new ones, and drinking. Looking back on the whole experience, I'm relieved it's over. I am exhausted from having to be in a constant state of motion to survive and socialize. Life has quieted down a significant amount since leaving, and I've let the absence of noise cleanse me of the chaos that had been brewing inside of me. What I'm left with is the shape of something I find vaguely familiar, bent and misshapen until it's barely recognizable. Like an animal, though, I recognize all of the other meta-properties of it. That used to be me. It still is.

I have a brand-new outlook on my life due to this. I would go as far to say that people don't struggle with anxiety today, instead, they struggle with discomfort. Life is inherently not comfortable. Life is unfiltered chaos, and the need for rigid structure is not your autism - it's your lack of willingness to push yourself further. Most people are capable of overcoming these aspects of themselves if they understand that growth doesn't come without pains. Hence, the term "growing pains". I am forever grateful for the experience that came from these struggles, self-induced or otherwise. Embracing impermanence is your only escape from stress.

_Life is like the wind. You can feel it, but you cannot hold onto it._
